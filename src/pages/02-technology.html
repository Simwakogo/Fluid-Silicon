Timing Health Monitoring

On-chip measurement, in production, for the life of the card.

The health layer measures timing slack on individual logic elements: LUTs, carry blocks and the links between them. A full sweep takes about a second; each link is resolved to under 20 ps.

What it measures

  • Per-element slack. Measured slack as a share of the clock period, per logic-element link, on every card.
  • Variation. Random, systematic and spatial variation shown separately, so a weak region has an address.
  • Aging. Each element tracked for the life of the card, fitted to its own temperature history.
  • Operating point. Voltage and frequency alongside timing, so a change can be checked against its effect.

The model behind the forecast

Each card's aging curve is fitted to its measured history and temperature, then projected to the alert threshold you set. Every sweep refines the fit.

In-Field Applications

Monitor, model, tune and, when needed, repair.

Monitoring feeds the aging model and the tuning; both feed back into monitoring, so every change is re-measured. Repair is the exception, not the routine: nothing in your design moves unless an element is degrading toward your threshold.

At the edge

Satellites, aircraft, radar and remote ground stations cannot wait for a fleet server. The health layer measures on the platform, projects a time-to-threshold per board, and scales voltage within the margin each device has measured.
