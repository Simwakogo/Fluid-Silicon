    About Fluid Silicon

    Every team should see the true limits of every chip it runs.

    We give the people who run critical systems a clear view of every chip's health and capability. Hardware you can see is hardware you can trust, and hardware you can trust does more with less.

    Our technology Careers

    The idea

    A different way to close the gap between design and deployment.

    Every chip is engineered once, for a worst-case scenario, then run that way for its entire life, regardless of how it actually performs once deployed. That is a reasonable approach given what has been possible to measure in the field. But as transistors shrink, the gap between how a chip was designed and how it actually behaves keeps widening, and it gets harder to see.

    What we build

    A health layer for the chip

    A small region beside your design that measures the timing of every logic element while the chip runs, so the chip can report its own condition. How it works

    What it changes

    Uncertainty becomes a number

    What each chip can sustain, where it is weak, how fast it is aging and when it will need attention, measured rather than assumed.

    Where it runs today

    AMD and Altera FPGAs

    Families from 28 nm to 10 nm class, integrated through each vendor's own flow, deployed on your site. Device support

    Team

    Built by the people who did the research.

    A founder who saw the biggest waste in deployed hardware as uncertainty, and a researcher who spent decades on the science underneath it.

    Nhlanhla Mavuso

    Co-Founder and CEO

    Nhlanhla led the research behind Fluid Silicon at the University of Pennsylvania, where he graduated from the Vagelos Integrated Program in Energy Research (VIPER), run jointly by Penn's School of Arts & Sciences and School of Engineering and Applied Science. Trained to look at systems by what they waste, he saw the biggest waste in deployed hardware as uncertainty: capability held back because nobody could see what each chip could really do.

    Where we're headed

    FPGAs are where we start.

    Fluid Silicon has demonstrated its technology on real commercial FPGAs. The next phase is putting it to work wherever chips have to be trusted, and growing the technical team to get there.

    1

    Today

    Per-element measurement on AMD and Altera parts already in production. Evaluations start read-only, on a sample of your cards.

    2

    Next

    The full loop, monitor, model, tune and repair, deployed across data centers, aerospace and defense, telecommunications and the other industries where reliability and performance matter most.

    3

    Further

    A chip that reports its own condition is useful wherever silicon has to be trusted. FPGAs are the first place it can be added without new silicon; they are the first step, not the last.

    How we work

    Measurement before opinion.

    A number measured on your chip is worth more than a guardband set for someone else's. Margin should cover measurement error, not uncertainty.

    Measured on the chip

    Every logic element, at speed, in production, with no downtime.

    Decisions on evidence

    Replacements, upgrades and retirements rest on data from the card itself.

    Your design, your control

    Nothing changes on a production system without your approval.

    Vendor-neutral by design

    One health metric that means the same thing on every part you run.
