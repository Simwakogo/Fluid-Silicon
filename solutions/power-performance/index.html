    Power & Performance

    Timing margins are set once, for the slowest chip at the hottest corner, because that is the only chip a vendor can guarantee. Fluid Silicon measures the margin each of your chips has and converts the difference into lower power and higher clocks, with the margin you specify kept in place.

    Adaptive Voltage Scaling

    Reduce power, safely.

    • Voltage set from timing measured on that chip, not a family datasheet.
    • Margin held only for measurement error and drift until the next sweep; each link is resolved to under 20 ps.
    • Every change scheduled, handshake-gated and re-measured within a second.
    • One policy on AMD and Altera parts.
    • Read-only first: the recommended operating point is visible before anything moves.

    Worked example

    10,000 cards at 75 W in a facility with a PUE of 1.2: every 1% of card power is about 79 MWh a year at the wall. The reduction available depends on the margin your chips carry today.

    Adaptive Frequency Scaling

    Raise the clock with a safety net.

    • Sustainable Fmax measured per chip over long periods, not a one-time lab figure.
    • Aging models lower the target before a chip drifts out of margin.
    • Changes run in your maintenance windows, with a handshake on every card.
    • Monitoring continues after the change.

    Guardrails

    Frequency rises only where measured margin supports it, by the amount that leaves your specified slack in place. A card aging toward the threshold is brought down before it reaches it.

    Per-Chip Sign-off

    One margin per chip.

    • Each chip's path-delay distribution, measured link by link.
    • Sign-off margin: measurement error plus drift, with each link resolved to under 20 ps.
    • Measuring more chips finds more tails; it adds no margin to the chips without them.
    • The same sign-off from 28 nm to 10 nm class.

    Why fleets carry margin today

    A vendor must guarantee timing for every chip it ships, so its model describes the worst one: defective, hot, at elevated voltage, at end of life. That is the right call for a vendor. It also means every card in your fleet carries that chip's margin.

    Read the margin explainer

    Fleet Power Policy

    One card, one server or the whole fleet.

    • Operating points coordinated per device, per server or fleet-wide.
    • Your thresholds, your windows, your approvals.
    • Telemetry in one vendor-neutral schema, into your existing monitoring.
    • Nothing is applied without a handshake.

    Read-only first

    Monitoring and modeling never change an operating point. Voltage and frequency scaling are separate steps enabled by policy, and you can stop at any step.

    FAQ

    Common questions.

    Sensor architecture, deployment mechanics and calibration methods are shared with evaluation partners under NDA.

    How much power can we save?

    It depends on the margin each chip carries today, which we measure before recommending anything. The worked example shows the arithmetic; an evaluation on a sample of your cards shows your number.

    Does tuning put timing at risk?

    Margin is kept for measurement error plus drift, every change is re-measured within a second, and aging models pull the operating point back before a chip drifts toward the threshold. Every change is scheduled and handshake-gated.

    Can we run voltage and frequency scaling together?

    Yes. Both read the same measurements. Most fleets start with voltage, which lowers power without changing throughput, and add frequency where a workload can use it.

    Which devices are supported?

    AMD 7-series, UltraScale and UltraScale+ today, with select Altera Agilex 7 devices. Stratix 10, Arria 10 and Versal AI Core are in development. See device support.

    Does it change our design?

    Your RTL is not changed. The health layer occupies a small region reserved beside your design, planned with your team during evaluation, and needs no JTAG exposure.

    What does it cost in area and delay?

    Under 1% of LUTs and under 5% of flip-flops on a production shell, under 2% delay impact, and no downtime to monitor.

    How does this relate to facility energy?

    Card power is paid for again in cooling and distribution, which is what PUE captures. Whatever you save on the card is multiplied at the wall.

    Is the margin you sign off the margin we keep?

    Yes. The measurement made at qualification continues in production, so the margin is checked for the life of the card rather than assumed once.
