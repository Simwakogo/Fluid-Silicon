    Degradation Prediction

    Which card fails next, and when.

    • Every element measured about once a second for the life of the card.
    • A per-card aging curve fitted to its temperature history.
    • A time-to-threshold that feeds replacement and capacity planning.
    • Cards aging faster than their neighbours flagged early.

    Prediction, not a schedule

    Replacing boards on a fixed schedule retires healthy cards and misses failing ones. A forecast per card replaces the schedule.

    Error Attribution

    A fault with an address.

    • Per-element slack history around the time of an error.
    • The weakest element on the card, and how long it has been weakening.
    • Evidence that separates a timing fault from a software or thermal event.
    • Reports your reliability team can attach to a ticket.

    Before the error travels

    An error in one card can take capacity offline or propagate downstream. Attribution shortens the path from symptom to cause.

    Outlier Detection

    The tails of the fleet.

    • Each card's weakest element compared across the fleet.
    • Systematic and random variation separated from aging.
    • Cards far below the family median surfaced first.
    • The same comparison on AMD and Altera parts.

    Variation at scale

    At 16, 14 and 7 nm, random variation alone exceeds 20%, on top of systematic variation across the die. Measuring more chips finds more tails.

    FAQ

    Common questions.

    Sensor architecture, deployment mechanics and calibration methods are shared with evaluation partners under NDA.

    What kinds of failure does this predict?

    Timing failures from variation and wear-out: logic that has slowed past its margin. It does not predict mechanical, power-supply or radiation events; it complements the measures you have for those.

    How is an error attributed?

    Every logic element has a measured slack history, so an error is checked against the elements that were weakest at the time. The result is evidence, not inference.

    Can it tell aging from a bad batch?

    Yes. Aging shows as a trend in a card's own history; a bad batch shows as an outlier at incoming characterization, before the card ages at all.

    Does monitoring affect our design's timing?

    The health layer adds under 2% delay and occupies under 1% of LUTs on a production shell. It does not interfere with the design's computation.

    How often is each card measured?

    A full sweep of every logic element takes about a second and runs while the design computes. The interval between sweeps is yours to set.

    Which devices are supported?

    AMD 7-series, UltraScale and UltraScale+ today, with select Altera Agilex 7 devices; more families in development. See device support.
