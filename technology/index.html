    Timing Health Monitoring

    On-chip measurement, in production, for the life of the card.

    The health layer measures timing slack on individual logic elements: LUTs, carry blocks and the links between them. A full sweep takes about a second; each link is resolved to under 20 ps.

    What it measures

    • Per-element slack. Measured slack as a share of the clock period, per logic-element link, on every card.
    • Variation. Random, systematic and spatial variation shown separately, so a weak region has an address.
    • Aging. Each element tracked for the life of the card, fitted to its own temperature history.
    • Operating point. Voltage and frequency alongside timing, so a change can be checked against its effect.

    Integration & Deployment

    Built for designs already in production.

    Integration asks one thing of your build: a small region reserved beside your design, sized and placed with your team during evaluation. Your RTL is not changed. Telemetry stays in your network.

    Reserve

    A small region of the device is reserved for the health layer: under 1% of LUTs and under 5% of flip-flops on a production shell.

    How much, and where, is planned with your team during evaluation. This is the one thing integration asks of your build; your logic, routing and I/O are not touched.

    Integrate

    The health layer is added beside a design that is already in production, through the vendor's own flow: AMD Vivado for AMD parts, Altera Quartus for Altera parts.

    No RTL changes, no new silicon, no JTAG exposure, and under 2% delay impact on your design.

    Deploy

    The platform runs on your site. Telemetry leaves each card in one vendor-neutral schema and lands in your existing monitoring; nothing leaves your network.

    Read-only is the default. Voltage scaling, frequency scaling and repair are enabled separately, by policy; every action is scheduled and handshake-gated.

    Characterization Applications

    Every card measured before service, and on the way out.

    The same measurement continues in production, so the margin you sign off is the margin you keep.

    Incoming

    Every LUT and carry block measured at speed on arrival, about a second per card, in staging.

    A per-card timing and variation report, read-only. Tail cards are known on day one.

    Weak-resource maps

    Measured slack mapped across the chip's logic elements, with random, systematic and spatial variation shown separately.

    The map guides your next place-and-route, so critical paths avoid weak regions, and is re-measured for the life of the card. Your current build is not touched.

    Exit

    Cards leaving service are measured the same way, so the exit report compares directly with the incoming one.

    Evidence for warranty, vendor feedback, and resale, reuse or retirement decisions.

    The model behind the forecast

    Each card's aging curve is fitted to its measured history and temperature, then projected to the alert threshold you set. Every sweep refines the fit.

    In-Field Applications

    Monitor, model, tune and, when needed, repair.

    Monitoring feeds the aging model and the tuning; both feed back into monitoring, so every change is re-measured. Repair is the exception, not the routine: nothing in your design moves unless an element is degrading toward your threshold.

    Monitor

    Every logic element swept in about a second while the design runs. One metric on every vendor: measured slack as a share of the clock period.

    Model

    An aging curve per card and per element, fitted to the card's own temperature history, with a predicted time-to-threshold. Outliers are flagged early.

    Tune

    Voltage and frequency set to what each chip can sustain, in windows you schedule, coordinated per device, per server or fleet-wide. Every change is re-measured.

    Repair

    Nothing in your design moves until an element degrades toward your threshold. Then only that path is moved to a healthy, faster resource, through your change process, and the card is re-measured within a second.

    At the edge

    Satellites, aircraft, radar and remote ground stations cannot wait for a fleet server. The health layer measures on the platform, projects a time-to-threshold per board, and scales voltage within the margin each device has measured.

    FAQ

    Common questions.

    Sensor architecture, deployment mechanics and calibration methods are shared with evaluation partners under NDA.

    What does the health layer measure?

    Timing slack on individual logic elements, including LUTs, carry blocks and the links between them, at operating speed. A full sweep of every element takes about a second, with each link resolved to under 20 ps.

    Does it change our design?

    Your RTL is not changed. The health layer occupies a small region reserved beside your design, sized and placed with your team during evaluation. Your logic, routing and I/O stay as built. Repair, if you enable it, moves only a path whose element is degrading; everything else stays put.

    What does it cost in resources?

    Under 1% of LUTs and under 5% of flip-flops on a production shell, with under 2% delay impact.

    Which devices and toolchains?

    AMD 7-series, UltraScale and UltraScale+ today, with select Altera Agilex 7 devices. Integration with each vendor's toolchain, including AMD Vivado and Altera Quartus, is handled by Fluid Silicon.

    Does it cover the whole lifecycle?

    Yes. Characterization at incoming, continuous monitoring in production, and characterization again at exit. Tuning and repair are opt-in steps in between.

    How is it different from monitoring built into an ASIC?

    Monitoring designed into an ASIC has to be there at tape-out. FPGAs are programmable, so the health layer is added beside a design already in your racks, with no new silicon and no RTL changes. FPGAs are where we start; the same approach extends to other reconfigurable and programmable silicon.

    Is it secure?

    The platform runs on your site. No JTAG port has to be exposed, nothing is written into your design's logic, telemetry stays in your network in one schema you control, and every action is opt-in, scheduled and handshake-gated. See security and integration.

    How does the sensing work?

    Sensor architecture, deployment mechanics and calibration methods are shared with evaluation partners under NDA. Ask for a technical session.
