Solutions

Chips are built to a fixed, idealized average. Their real-world condition tells a different story.

As chips heat, cool, and age, and due to natural variation from manufacturing, their real performance and energy characteristics drift from the assumptions they were designed against. Conventional design ignores this and simply overengineers for the worst case, wasting energy and performance across every chip in the fleet.

How It Works

A closed loop system on unmodified silicon that can help reconfigurable chips to monitor themselves and adapt to their unique set of conditions, and further repair themselves.

Why This Matters

A more efficient world starts with what we've already built.

Our vision: infrastructure that makes computing adaptable, evolvable, and sustainable, built for the demands of AI and beyond.

Electricity demand is growing faster than it has in decades, and not from one source. Data centers, telecom networks, transportation, aerospace and satellite systems, and everyday infrastructure are all drawing more power as the world runs more of itself through computing. The instinct across every one of these industries is the same: build more capacity, more infrastructure, more hardware.

We think there's a faster lever sitting mostly untouched: the hardware already deployed today isn't running anywhere close to its real potential. Every chip in the field, whether it's in a data center, a cell tower, or a satellite, is held back by conservative, worst-case design. It's tuned for the chip it might be, not the one it actually is.

Fluid Silicon exists to close that gap, wherever it shows up. Not by replacing the hardware, but by letting it tell you what it's actually capable of, and giving it room to run there. Conserving the energy we already generate, and getting more out of the compute and infrastructure we already have, is one of the fastest paths to a more efficient world, across every industry that depends on it.
